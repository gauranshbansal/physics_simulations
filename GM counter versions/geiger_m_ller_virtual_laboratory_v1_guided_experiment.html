GEIGER-MÜLLER DETECTOR PRINCIPLES & ORIENTATION GUIDE

1. DETECTOR ANATOMY & GEOMETRY

Central Wire (Anode +): A thin tungsten wire of radius $a = 0.05\,\text{mm}$ maintained at positive high voltage ($V_0 \approx 900\,\text{V}$). The cylindrical geometry concentrates an intense electric field $E(r) \propto 1/r$ directly at the wire surface.
Outer Cylinder (Cathode −): Metal cylindrical wall of radius $b = 10.0\,\text{mm}$ at ground potential. Collects positive ions and acts as a physical barrier and electrical shield.
Mica Entrance Window ($1.5\,\text{mg/cm}^2$): Ultra-thin end window permitting low-penetrating radiation (alphas, low-energy betas) to enter the internal gas volume without breaking vacuum/gas containment.
Fill Gas (Argon + Halogen Quench): $90\%$ Argon gas at low pressure ($0.10\,\text{atm}$) serves as the primary ionizing medium ($I_p = 15.8\,\text{eV}$), mixed with $10\%$ Bromine ($Br_2$) quench vapor.

2. PARTICLE VOCABULARY & VISUAL IDENTIFIERS

Beta ($\beta^-$): Fast incoming energetic electron.
Secondary Electron ($e^-$): Freed by collision; drifts inward.
Argon Ion ($Ar^+$): Heavy positive ion; drifts outward.
UV Photon ($\gamma_{uv}$): Emitted by excited gas; spreads discharge.
Quench Molecule ($Q^+$): Neutralizes $Ar^+$ without emitting secondary $e^-$.
Alpha ($\alpha$): Heavy $+2e$ helium nucleus.

3. EXPERIMENTAL PARAMETERS & CHEMISTRY

Distance ($d$): The physical spacing measured between the radiation emitter source and the detector's mica entrance window. Increasing $d$ reduces the geometric solid angle $\Omega \approx A / (4\pi d^2)$, causing count rate to follow the inverse square law.
Quenching Chemistry: When $Ar^+$ ions impact the cathode wall, their high ionization potential ($15.8\,\text{eV}$) can eject secondary electrons, creating continuous false afterpulses. Halogen ($Br_2$, $I_p = 10.5\,\text{eV}$) transfers charge: $Ar^+ + Br_2 \to Ar + Br_2^+$. $Br_2^+$ dissociates harmlessly at the wall into neutral atoms, which spontaneously recombine.
Dead Time ($\tau_d \approx 65\,\mu\text{s}$): The positive ion sheath shields the anode wire, choking the electric field. Until ions drift far enough outward, new incoming particles cannot ignite an avalanche and are completely lost.
Applied Voltage ($V_0$): At $\sim 900\,\text{V}$ (Plateau), all pulses have uniform amplitude ($~5\,\text{V}$) regardless of the initial radiation type or energy, providing maximum counting stability.

4. THE PHYSICAL DETECTION CHAIN

Radiation $\to$ Primary Ionization $\to$ Inward Electron Acceleration $\to$ Townsend Avalanche $\to$ Space Charge Sheath $\to$ Voltage Pulse $\to$ Dead Time $\to$ Recovery

1. Radiation creates $Ar^+ + e^-$ $\to$ 2. $e^-$ accelerates into high $E(r)$ wire field $\to$ 3. Massive avalanche multiplication ($10^9$ electrons) $\to$ 4. Pulse induced on wire $\to$ 5. Slow ion sheath chokes field (Dead Time) $\to$ 6. Ions neutralize at cathode (Ready again).
